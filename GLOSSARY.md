# A-10 Cuba! — flight, cockpit, and combat glossary

An explanation of the aviation, military, and technical vocabulary encountered
in **Parsoft's 1996 A-10 Cuba!**, its cockpit, loadouts, missions, and
[keyboard reference](A-10-Cuba-keyboard-shortcuts.md). This is written for
someone playing the game, not for someone already familiar with military
aviation. Related entries are grouped by subject and alphabetized within each
group.

**Period matters:** the game models an A-10 of the 1990s, not the much later
upgraded **A-10C**. Terms such as GPS-guided JDAMs, SADL, and modern targeting
pods found in contemporary A-10 guides should not be read back into the game.
Some entries describe real-world equipment or tactics used to make sense of
the simulation; that alone does **not** establish that every feature is
implemented or controllable in the game.

## Aircraft and flight

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **A-10 / A-10A Thunderbolt II** | US twin-engine, single-seat attack aircraft designed for close air support. The A-10 is nicknamed the *Warthog* or *Hog*. |
| **Acceleration** | Change in speed over time; on the ground it depends on engine thrust, drag, and brakes. |
| **Afterburner** | Extra fuel burned in a jet exhaust to increase thrust. **The A-10's turbofans do not have afterburners.** |
| **Ailerons** | Movable control surfaces on the wings that command *roll* (bank). |
| **Airbrake / speed brake** | Surface extended to increase drag and slow the aircraft in flight. Distinct from wheel brakes on the ground; both are associated with the game's brake command. |
| **Airspeed** | Speed relative to the surrounding air, not speed across the terrain; normally expressed in knots. |
| **Altitude** | Height above a reference such as mean sea level (barometric) or nearby ground (radar altitude). |
| **Angle of attack (AoA)** | Angle between the wing's reference line and the oncoming airflow. High AoA can cause a stall even at a positive pitch angle. |
| **Attitude** | Orientation of the aircraft: pitch, roll, and usually heading/yaw, rather than its location. |
| **Bank** | Tilt left or right produced by roll; used in turns. |
| **Compressor stall** | Disruption of airflow inside a jet engine compressor; may cause banging, loss of thrust, or engine damage. Different from an aerodynamic wing stall. |
| **Drag** | Aerodynamic force opposing movement through the air. Gear, flaps, and brakes generally increase it. |
| **Elevator** | Tail control surface used primarily to change pitch, raising or lowering the nose. |
| **Engine-out** | Flying with one engine stopped or inoperative; the remaining engine can produce asymmetric thrust. |
| **Flaps** | Wing surfaces deployed to increase lift (and drag), typically for takeoff or landing; the game changes flap position in 10° steps. |
| **Glide / glideslope** | Unpowered descent / the intended descent path toward a runway. Do not confuse the latter with the ILS electronic glideslope signal. |
| **Ground speed** | Speed over the ground; differs from airspeed in wind. |
| **Heading** | Compass direction toward which the aircraft's nose points, conventionally in degrees clockwise from north. |
| **IAS (indicated airspeed)** | Airspeed read from the pitot-static instruments, uncorrected for several measurement effects. |
| **Kn / kt (knot)** | Nautical mile per hour, about 1.852 km/h. Standard aviation speed unit. |
| **Landing gear** | Nose and main wheels, struts, and associated mechanisms; retractable in flight. |
| **Lift** | Aerodynamic force roughly perpendicular to the airflow; holds up the aircraft in normal flight. |
| **Mach number** | Aircraft speed divided by the local speed of sound; Mach 1 is the speed of sound. |
| **Nosewheel steering** | Turning the nose gear wheel while taxiing; effectiveness/engagement can differ from rudder steering as speed increases. |
| **Pitch** | Nose-up or nose-down rotation around the lateral axis. |
| **Roll** | Rotation around the nose-to-tail axis; controlled chiefly by the ailerons. |
| **Rudder** | Vertical-tail control that produces yaw (nose left/right). In flight it is not the same as banking to turn. |
| **SAS (Stability Augmentation System)** | Flight-control assistance that damps unwanted motion. It is separate from the autopilot, which attempts to hold or follow a flight condition. |
| **Stall (aerodynamic)** | Loss of lift due to excessive wing angle of attack; it does not mean that an engine has stopped. |
| **Taxi / taxiway** | Moving an aircraft on the ground under its own power / the paved path between parking and runway. |
| **TF34** | General Electric turbofan engine type used in the A-10; the aircraft has two. |
| **Throttle** | Control of engine power; reducing it is not equivalent to applying wheel or speed brakes. |
| **Trim** | Adjustment that reduces the need for continuous control input to hold a desired attitude. Elevator trim and rudder trim are distinct from elevator/rudder movement. |
| **Turbofan** | Jet engine that produces thrust from both a fan-driven bypass stream and the hot exhaust. |
| **Yaw** | Nose-left or nose-right rotation around the vertical axis. |

## Navigation, cockpit, and instruments

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **ADI (Attitude Director Indicator)** | Instrument showing aircraft pitch/bank relative to the horizon; sometimes called the artificial horizon. |
| **AGL (Above Ground Level)** | Altitude above the terrain directly below, useful for low-level flight. Contrast **MSL**. |
| **Autopilot** | System that automatically controls part of the flight path or attitude. In the game it can work with route/navigation settings; it is not an automatic combat pilot. |
| **BARO / barometric altitude** | Altitude inferred from air pressure using an altimeter setting. Usually referenced to mean sea level rather than the terrain below. |
| **Bearing** | Direction from one location to another; may be relative to the nose or referenced to north. |
| **Cockpit** | Pilot's compartment, with the HUD, instruments, switches, and controls. Many controls in A-10 Cuba! are also mouse-clickable. |
| **Compass rose** | Circular scale of headings around an instrument or map. |
| **Course** | Desired direction of travel along a route; it may differ from heading when correcting for wind. |
| **ECM (Electronic Countermeasures)** | Equipment intended to interfere with enemy detection or tracking, especially radar. Can include a jamming pod. Distinct from deploying chaff or flares. |
| **HUD (Head-Up Display)** | Flight and aiming symbology projected in front of the pilot so the outside view remains visible. |
| **HUD color / intensity** | Display tint / brightness. Brightness is a visibility setting, not a change in the weapon mode. |
| **HUD mode** | A selected set of HUD symbols and aiming calculations; the game includes **NAV**, **PAVE/PAV**, and **CCIP** modes. |
| **ILS (Instrument Landing System)** | Real-world radio landing guidance giving approach alignment and descent information. Do not assume a fully modeled ILS in this game. |
| **Instrument panel** | Conventional gauges, indicators, and switches surrounding or below the HUD. |
| **MFD (Multi-Function Display)** | Screen capable of showing different pages, such as TV weapon imagery or damage/status information. “MFD off/TV/damage” are display selections, not weapons. |
| **MSL (Mean Sea Level)** | Height relative to an average sea-level datum. An aircraft at 1,000 ft MSL may be much less than 1,000 ft above a hill. |
| **NAV** | HUD navigation mode, showing information intended to help fly a course/route rather than aiming unguided ordnance. |
| **Pave Penny** | A passive laser-spot *receiver/tracker* mounted on historical A-10s. It detects laser light reflected from a target designated by someone else; **it does not shine its own targeting laser**. |
| **PAVE / PAV** | The game's HUD mode associated with laser-marked targets/Pave Penny symbology. Do not confuse it with a general-purpose onboard laser designator. |
| **RAD / radar altitude** | Height above terrain measured by a radar altimeter; the HUD can switch between RAD and BARO readouts. This is not an air-to-air search radar. |
| **RWR (Radar Warning Receiver)** | Passive sensor/display warning of radar emissions from other aircraft or ground-based threats. An RWR warning does not necessarily mean a missile has been launched. |
| **RWR hand-off** | Selecting/handing off a radar threat indication for another cockpit function; the exact game's selection behavior needs checking in its help file. |
| **TACAN (Tactical Air Navigation)** | Military radio navigation system providing bearing and distance to a beacon. In the game, navigation/autopilot behavior also uses waypoints. |
| **Tactical map** | Game map of mission area, route, and relevant units/objectives; opened from flight with Escape. |
| **TV (television) seeker/display** | Visible-light camera picture used to aim or track a TV-guided weapon, such as certain Maverick variants. |
| **Waypoint / steerpoint** | Saved point along a route; selecting the next point changes the navigation reference. |

## Weapons and their management

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **AGM** | US designation prefix for an *air-to-ground missile*; e.g. AGM-65. |
| **AGM-65 Maverick** | Guided air-to-ground missile family for vehicles and other point targets. Different variants use TV, infrared, or laser-related guidance. |
| **AGM-65B / -65D / -65E** | Maverick variants: B uses a TV/electro-optical seeker, D an imaging infrared seeker, E laser guidance. Variant availability depends on the mission/loadout. |
| **AIM** | US designation prefix for an *air-intercept missile*, a type of air-to-air guided missile. |
| **AIM-9 Sidewinder** | Infrared-homing air-to-air missile. Windows v1.2 adds Sidewinders to certain multiplayer loadouts. |
| **Arm / disarm** | Permit or inhibit weapons release. The game's **master arm** switch is a safety control; station selection is a separate action. |
| **Avenger / GAU-8/A** | A-10's seven-barrel 30 mm rotary cannon. Its high/low firing-rate setting is not the trigger itself. |
| **BLU** | US munitions nomenclature prefix for a *Bomb Live Unit*, often a bomb or submunition component. |
| **Bomb** | Air-dropped munition; may be unguided (“dumb”) or guided. A higher release altitude typically gives a weapon more time to fall or steer. |
| **CBU (Cluster Bomb Unit)** | Dispenser that releases many smaller submunitions across an area, as opposed to a single unitary bomb. |
| **CCIP (Continuously Computed Impact Point)** | Aiming mode that predicts where an unguided weapon would hit if released under current flight conditions and marks the point on the HUD. |
| **Chaff** | Small radar-reflecting strips/fibers released to confuse radar-guided threats. Usually of little use against a purely infrared seeker. |
| **Countermeasure** | Defensive device or action intended to defeat detection, tracking, or missile guidance; includes ECM, chaff, and flares. |
| **ECM pod** | External electronic-jamming equipment carried on a weapon station; a station may hold equipment rather than a weapon. |
| **Flare** | Bright infrared decoy released to distract heat-seeking missiles. Not equivalent to chaff. |
| **GAU** | US designation for certain aircraft guns; **GAU-8/A** is the Avenger cannon. |
| **Hardpoint / pylon / station** | Physical aircraft attachment point / structure / numbered loadout selection. The A-10 has 11 external stations, but not every station is suitable for every weapon. |
| **IR (infrared)** | Radiation outside the red end of visible light; an IR-seeking weapon detects target heat patterns. |
| **Jettison / JETT** | Deliberately drop external stores, usually without employing them normally. The game also has a jettison release mode. |
| **LAU** | Common US designation prefix for aircraft launchers; often used for rocket pods. |
| **Laser designation** | A target illuminated by a coded laser from an aircraft or ground observer, enabling laser-seeking weapons/sensors to find it. |
| **LGB (Laser-Guided Bomb)** | Bomb that uses laser reflections to correct its path toward a designated target; examples are Paveway-equipped Mk-series bombs. |
| **Lock / target lock** | A seeker or game targeting system has selected/tracked an object. A HUD target selection and a weapon seeker's own lock are not always the same thing. |
| **Master arm** | Main cockpit safety switch enabling weapons employment. Arming the aircraft does not automatically pick a station. |
| **Mk 82 / Mk 84** | US general-purpose bomb bodies: nominally 500 lb and 2,000 lb classes, respectively. Guidance kits can turn some Mk-series bombs into guided weapons. |
| **Ordnance / stores** | Carried weapons / everything suspended on external stations, including tanks or pods. “Stores” need not be explosive. |
| **Paveway** | Family of laser-guidance kits/bombs. Not the same thing as the A-10's **Pave Penny** laser receiver. |
| **Pipper / reticle** | HUD aiming marker or crosshair showing a predicted impact point or line of sight. |
| **Release cue** | HUD indication that conditions for releasing a selected weapon are satisfied. |
| **Release mode** | Rule determining *how many* stores are released and in what pattern; separate from the selected weapon type. |
| **RIP / ripple** | Repeated weapon releases with a selected delay between them. In game programming, `RIP SGL` ripples one store at a time and `RIP PRS` ripples pairs. |
| **Ripple count / quantity** | Number of release events or stores configured for a ripple sequence. Interpret any in-game count in the context of its mode. |
| **Ripple interval** | Time between releases; the game's programming accepts a two-digit value in **10 ms units** (e.g. `25` means 250 ms). |
| **Rocket pod** | External launcher holding multiple unguided rockets. Rockets are powered in flight, unlike free-fall bombs. |
| **Rockeye** | Common name for the Mk 20 air-delivered anti-armor cluster weapon. |
| **SGL / PRS** | *Single* / *pairs*: release one store / a pair. Often used with either immediate or ripple modes. |
| **Station selection** | Enabling one or several weapon stations so the release control will use their stores. `F1`–`F11` refer to stations, not necessarily to 11 weapon types. |
| **TV-guided** | Guided by a television/electro-optical picture, allowing the operator or weapon seeker to identify a target. |

## Missions, threats, and tactics

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **AAA (Anti-Aircraft Artillery)** | Guns intended to shoot down aircraft; often a short-range low-level threat. |
| **Air defense / air-defense suppression** | Weapons and sensors defending against aircraft / attacks that reduce their effectiveness. |
| **CAS (Close Air Support)** | Aircraft attacks coordinated with friendly ground forces near enemy troops. The A-10's central mission. |
| **Dogfight** | Maneuvering engagement between aircraft, usually at relatively short range. |
| **Escort** | Mission to protect a friendly aircraft or formation from threats. |
| **FAC (Forward Air Controller)** | Person or aircraft directing/coordinating air attacks for ground forces, often including target marking. |
| **Friendly / hostile / neutral** | Whether an object belongs to allied forces, an enemy, or neither. The game has separate external friendly/enemy views. |
| **Interdiction** | Strikes against enemy forces or supplies before they reach or affect the immediate battle. |
| **Loiter** | Remain near an area while waiting for targets, orders, or an opportunity to support troops. |
| **Mission briefing** | Preflight description of objectives, threats, and route; different from real-time orders in flight. |
| **NOE (Nap-of-the-Earth)** | Very low-altitude flying that uses terrain to reduce exposure to enemy detection and fire. |
| **SAM (Surface-to-Air Missile)** | Guided missile launched from the ground toward aircraft; can use radar or infrared guidance. |
| **SEAD (Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses)** | Operations aimed at neutralizing, destroying, or temporarily disrupting enemy air-defense systems. |
| **Sortie** | One flight of an aircraft on a mission, generally from takeoff through return/landing. |
| **Threat emitter** | Radar or other emitting system that an RWR might detect and identify. |
| **Wingman / formation** | Friendly aircraft/pilot accompanying the lead / a coordinated arrangement of multiple aircraft. |

## Simulation and multiplayer terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **AI (Artificial Intelligence)** | Game-controlled pilots/units and their decision making. Does not mean a modern language model. |
| **Cooley/Coolie hat** | Hat-shaped multi-direction switch on a joystick used for view or other controls; spelling varies across old documentation. |
| **Dead zone** | Small controller movement range ignored to avoid drift around center. |
| **Force feedback** | Physical forces or vibration supplied by a compatible controller, if supported by hardware and software. |
| **HOTAS (Hands On Throttle And Stick)** | Control arrangement providing frequently used functions on the throttle and joystick instead of the keyboard. |
| **Instant message / taunt** | Preconfigured multiplayer text message / prerecorded voice sound, sent with v1.2 network shortcuts. |
| **Joystick axis** | Continuously varying input, such as pitch, roll, rudder, or throttle, as distinct from a button press. |
| **Prediction / extrapolation** | Estimating where another networked aircraft will move while waiting for new position updates. v1.2 exposes a prediction setting. |
| **Score / rearm / reload** | Network-game results and the option to equip a surviving aircraft again. Not the same as resetting an entire mission. |
